# Measurement round, 2026-11-22

Encoder-only tuning ended with the 2026-11-21 round (`overnight-2026-11-21.md`). This round
measures what decoder-side changes could still gain before any is built: a longer gate, an exact
offline price for context changes, and oracles for prediction tools. Negative numbers are fewer
bits at equal quality.

## Tools added

- **Gate v2** (`tools/gate.py`, see `README.md`): `--long` (300 frames of six clips), the
  realtime and light tiers, single-thread decode speed per encode, optional ColorVideoVDP, and the
  transmit delay of every CBR encode.
- **`vdwt ctx-sim`**: replays every written band's and motion section's symbols through exact
  copies of the coder's adaptive distributions, carried and reset as the encoder carries them,
  and prices a context refinement without changing the format. Its baseline is within 0.5% of
  the real coded size.
- **`tools/pred_oracle.py`**: applies ideal prediction tools to the encoder's real prediction and
  prices them against the P-frames' band bytes.
- `vdwt pred-dump` had been writing the previous reconstruction instead of the prediction; fixed.

## The reference lines, read with their delay

| codec | mean / worst transmit queue on the nominal link (frames) |
|---|---|
| VDWT | ≤ 1.5 (3 at ultraheavy), by construction |
| x265 `--tune zerolatency`, 3-frame VBV | 2.8 / 3.6 (long set), 2.5 / 3.1 (120 frames) |
| SVT-AV1 preset 8, `--pred-struct 1 --rc 2`, 3-frame buffer | **36.7 / 97** (long set), 21.2 / 114 (120 frames) |

SVT-AV1's low-delay CBR ignores `--buf-sz`, `--buf-initial-sz`, `--buf-optimal-sz` and
`--overshoot-pct`: on FourPeople at 300 kbit/s a frame waits up to 1.6 s. Its talking-head lead
(2–3 dB at 300 kbit/s) comes from its temporal-layer qp cascade, whose base frames are about ten
average frames each; at fixed CRF, flattening the layer offsets costs it about 2 dB on Kristen.
That lead is not available to a codec that holds a few frames of delay. At VDWT's latency the
comparable reference is x265.

## Where VDWT stands on the long set (1593431, CBR, mean of 6 clips, PSNR / VMAF BD-rate)

| | vs x265 | vs SVT-AV1 |
|---|---|---|
| VDWT realtime | +23.9 / +7.3% | +114 / +86% |
| VDWT medium | +3.9 / +1.4% | +64 / +75% |
| VDWT ultraheavy | **−9.2 / −8.3%** | +34 / +51% |

The 120-frame gate (ultraheavy −1.6 / −0.5% against x265) understated VDWT: on 2-second clips
the opening intra frame, held to its delay-limited budget, weighs more than in a real stream.
Under CBR the intra frame is about 1% of a 300-frame stream. Against SVT-AV1, VDWT leads on
crowd_run, ducks and Luckfox (PSNR) and trails on the talking heads and old_town_cross.

## What the offline tools ruled out

| idea | best result | |
|---|---|---|
| Band contexts: node flags by left/above node, ρ by left × above pair, exponent by left/above, chroma from co-located luma, magnitude and mantissa by neighbours, temporal ρ | −0.39% of the stream; most ≤ 0.1% | out |
| Motion contexts: y class from x class, class from neighbour spread, mode and split from the previous frame, sign and mantissa bit in context | ≤ 0.15% of the stream | out |
| A "same motion as the last frame" root flag | repeating roots cost only 5–9% of motion bits on talking heads | out |
| Brightness compensation (frame gain/offset, smooth offset or gain fields) | ≤ 0.05 dB prediction; side information exceeds the saving | out |
| A second hypothesis from the previous picture | ≈ 0 | out |
| Higher motion λ (×2, ×4) or a 64-px grid on talking heads | +2 to +47% | out: motion bits are earned |

Upper bound for a richer motion model: a dense 8×8 whole-pel match with free side information
improves the prediction by 0.4–1.7 dB and saves 5–15% of the P-frame residual (1–7% of the
stream at fixed qp). Real side information would take much of that.

## Low-delay temporal layers (built, not shipped)

Reference slots (a format change) let a P-frame predict from the last frame of a lower layer, so
the top layer is never a reference and codes coarser, as SVT-AV1 does, without added latency.
It decodes exactly but fails the gate (medium, long set):

| | fixed qp | CBR |
|---|---|---|
| 1 layer | −0.5 / +6.0% | +9.2 / +12.2% |
| 2 layers | +1.4 / +13.5% | +28.9 / +32.4% |

old_town_cross gains 19–28% at fixed qp and talking heads 3–5%; everything else loses.
Predicting from two to four frames back raises Kristen's motion share from 34% to 50% of the
stream and costs 1 dB under CBR even without a qp step: VDWT's per-node vectors make long-range
prediction expensive where AV1's skip and merge modes make it nearly free.

## Motion is the cost on static-camera content

Motion takes 5–27% of the stream across the gate under CBR and 34–44% on talking heads at fixed
qp; every bit of it is earned. That is what makes both finer motion and long-range references
expensive, and it is the next thing to study: a block-free motion-model oracle with its side
information priced.

## Also today

`vdwt encode` streams its input: it reads six frames ahead of the encoder and writes the stream
as it is coded, so a pipe starts at once and memory stays at about 60 MB however long it runs
(it held every frame before, 1.4 MB per 720p frame). Streams are unchanged.
